From Accumulation to Application

The Liahona Food Program: designing a globally comprehensive member food security program funded by Ensign Peak investment returns.

Summary

The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints manages one of the largest privately held investment portfolios in the United States through its subsidiary, Ensign Peak Advisors. Independent assessments place total Church investment assets near $180 billion at the midpoint, generating roughly $9.0 billion a year under a standard 5% endowment spending model. Yet the welfare infrastructure serving members who experience food insecurity remains largely unchanged from its mid-twentieth-century design, relying on Bishop's Storehouses, in-kind transfers, and discretionary local judgment rather than systematic, rights-based assistance.

This brief proposes the Liahona Food Program: a SNAP-modeled, income-tested food benefit funded entirely from Ensign Peak investment income and administered through an independent Office of Member Welfare Accountability. Benefits are calibrated to SNAP FY2026 maximum allotments and a 130% federal-poverty-level gross-income threshold, applied across 38 countries and regions covering about 90% of the Church's 17.89 million members, with a wage-ratio multiplier scaling benefits to local economic conditions.

At full global implementation the program would serve approximately 4.78 million eligible members at an annual cost of $3.43 billion — 38% of estimated annual returns at the midpoint. Modeled conservatively against the SNAP literature and international cash transfer research, it would lift roughly 584,000 members out of poverty each year at a cost of $5,868 per person, competitive with the most efficient anti-poverty programs in the world. Even under a conservative $124 billion asset scenario the program consumes only 55% of returns. The principal portfolio is preserved in full, and no tithing revenue is required.

Key assumptions

  • Ensign Peak assets-under-management midpoint of $180 billion (Widow's Mite consensus for total Church investment assets, 2023–24)
  • Current LDS humanitarian baseline of $1.45 billion (2024 official figure)
  • Benefit structure aligned with SNAP FY2026 maximum allotments
  • Eligibility threshold of 130% of the local poverty level, consistent with SNAP gross income limits
  • Modeled per-recipient average benefit of $190 per month, matching SNAP's actual average
  • Global membership of 17.89 million per the official 2025 statistical report
